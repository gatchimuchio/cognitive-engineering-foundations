# Part I. Full English Translation of the Japanese Canonical Original

# 1. Standard problem

General relativity treats gravity as dynamic spacetime geometry.

Quantum theory treats states, observables, probability, time evolution, and related structures in quantum form.

In domains where both gravitational and quantum effects matter, it has been considered necessary to have a theory capable of handling both.

Standard literature itself also discusses the difficulty of reconciling the role of “time” in general relativity with the role of time in ordinary quantum theory as the “problem of time” in quantum gravity.

This paper does not deny that non-alignment.

It asks:

> **From the observation of non-alignment, how can one already conclude that “one unified theory must exist”?**

---

# 2. Audit the establishment of the problem before solving it

The ordinary question is:

```text
How can general relativity and quantum theory be unified?
```

Cognitive Engineering returns one level upstream.

```text
Why were they judged to be targets for unification?
↓
What do the two theories treat as the same target?
↓
Do identically named terms such as “time,” “space,” “state,” and “observation” play the same role?
↓
What observation or logic guarantees identity or continuity?
↓
Only then does “cannot be unified” become a closed problem
```

In other words:

> **Close the problem setting before searching for a solution.**

---

# 3. The same word does not guarantee the same target

In general relativity, spacetime geometry itself is dynamic and tied to gravity.

In ordinary quantum theory, time, background, and observation conditions appear in different roles when describing time evolution and measurement of quantum states.

Therefore, do not fix:

```text
“time” in GR
=
“time” in quantum theory
```

or:

```text
“space / spacetime” in GR
=
the “space” used to describe states in quantum theory
```

merely because the same word is used.

The word “space” can refer to different targets such as:

- physical location / geometry,
- state space,
- Hilbert space,
- configuration space,
- background spacetime.

This is not merely a terminology problem.

> **If different targets are given the same name, treated as the same target, and then required to unify, the problem itself can be artificially created.**

---

# 4. General relativity and quantum theory have different observation worlds

Cognitive Engineering treats a theory not only as equations but as a cognitive world containing:

- observing subject,
- target,
- state,
- time,
- space,
- causality,
- measurement,
- background,
- identity,
- judgment rules.

The division of roles among these elements need not be the same in general relativity and quantum theory.

Therefore:

> **Two theories appearing to address the same nature is different from the two theories describing the same target structure.**

---

# 5. What non-alignment means

If the two current theories do not compose consistently, what can be said with certainty is:

> **the current combination does not close.**

From that alone, the following do not automatically follow:

```text
a unified theory necessarily exists
```

```text
they should be unified while preserving the current concepts of “time” and “space”
```

```text
if one or both theories are modified long enough, they will necessarily become one
```

From Cognitive Engineering, non-alignment can be treated as a mismatch signal from the world saying:

> **reopen target identity, coordinates, relations, and the question itself.**

---

# 6. Decompose the word “unification”

“Unification” in quantum gravity can mix at least several different meanings:

1. integrate into one mathematical formalism,
2. reduce to one set of basic variables,
3. unify into one ontology,
4. predict the same observation domain without contradiction,
5. recover one theory as a limit of the other,
6. construct a translation map between different theories.

These are not identical.

Cognitive Engineering first fixes:

> **What counts as successful unification?**

If predictive consistency alone is the purpose, ontological monism may be unnecessary.

If ontological monism is required, grounds for requiring it must be shown separately.

---

# 7. Rewriting the quantum-gravity problem through X / R / M

Using the representative Projection of the Trinity Principle:

### X

- general relativity,
- quantum theory,
- the observational domain in which both are said to apply,
- the definitions of time, space, state, measurement, and related terms inside each theory.

### R

- correspondences,
- limit relations,
- transformations,
- shared observables,
- locations of inconsistency,
- maps by which targets are treated as identical.

### M

- what is called unification,
- what is treated as the same target,
- which observations serve as shared judgment criteria,
- which inconsistency counts as FAIL,
- where SUSPEND occurs.

If M is not fixed, the question:

```text
“solve quantum gravity”
```

may itself remain unclosed.

---

# 8. The problem of time is also a warning from Cognitive Engineering

Standard quantum-gravity literature discusses problems arising because “time” has different roles in general relativity and ordinary quantum theory.

This matters from Cognitive Engineering.

> **The same word can carry different functions in different theoretical worlds.**

If so, word and target must be separated first.

Before asking “how should time be quantized?”, close:

> **Which time is being referred to?**

---

# 9. What this paper does not claim

This paper does not claim:

- quantum gravity is impossible,
- a unified theory does not exist,
- general relativity is wrong,
- quantum theory is wrong,
- physicists never consider target identity.

It claims only:

> **The observed fact that current theories cannot be unified does not, by itself, prove the identity of the targets to be unified or the existence of a unified theory.**

---

# 10. Conditions for the problem to hold

To strongly close quantum gravity as one problem, at least the following are required:

1. show an observation domain in which both theories are simultaneously required,
2. show which observables must be shared,
3. show grounds for treating the targets as identical,
4. show correspondences among time, space, state, and measurement,
5. fix what counts as success of unification,
6. if ontological unification is required, separately show why it is necessary.

Only after these are closed does it become clear:

> **what must actually be solved.**

---

# 11. Relation to 神の領域原理（仮）

General relativity and quantum theory are both extremely powerful world-models constructed after cognition.

Their non-alignment is not automatically converted into the ontology:

```text
one unified theory is hidden inside the world-itself
```

Under observation conditions that require unification, the unification problem may be pursued strongly and locally.

But the problem setting, target identity, and success conditions remain reopenable.

---

# 12. Refutation and reopening conditions

This paper is re-audited at least if:

- it is uniquely shown observationally or logically, rather than by word identity, that general relativity and quantum theory describe the same target structure,
- the existence of a unified theory is independently derived,
- it is shown that the target-identity audit raised here has already been fully resolved in existing quantum-gravity research,
- the separation of different “time” or “space” is shown to be wrong and treating them as the same target has greater explanatory power.

---

# 13. Conclusion

The question this paper asks about quantum gravity is not:

> **How can they be unified?**

Before that:

> **Why does the unification problem hold? What is being treated as the same target and unified?**

The fact that current general relativity and quantum theory do not align shows that the current combination does not close.

That is an important observation.

But:

> **Failure to close is not proof that one unified theory must exist.**

Before searching for a solution, Cognitive Engineering re-audits the question, target, coordinates, identity, and success conditions.
